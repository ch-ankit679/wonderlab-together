# WonderLab Together — school pitch (6.0)

## The classroom problem

A platform that mainly offers games or quizzes can make a class look engaged without ensuring that students have learned every important idea in a chapter. Students need explanations before assessment, and digital activities should not add accounts, server maintenance or unnecessary preparation.

## The proposal

WonderLab Together is a student-facing science learning adventure. An adult selects one of two chapter pathways, then students follow six guided concept lessons and a separate collaborative team challenge. Pip voices the learner-facing journey: welcomes the class, narrates concepts and examples, reads every question and answer choice, explains lab observations, offers clues and gives feedback. The adult selects the chapter, pace and team count, then operates the shared screen rather than narrating the lesson.

## What students experience

- **Complete learning sequence:** six concept lessons in each chapter rather than a small set of disconnected activities.
- **Direct explanations:** every lesson teaches the idea in accessible student-facing language.
- **Realistic visual learning: larger cell cutaways, membrane bilayer and transport diagrams, moving bridge and beaker scenes, and editable labels checked against science references.
- **Multimedia learning:** animated diagrams, selectable structures, automatic Pip voice guidance where device speech is supported and interactive science models.
- **Learning by exploring:** students predict, change a control, observe the result and connect it to the explanation.
- **Check understanding:** a lesson-level concept check follows every lesson; Wonder League provides a separate 12-question application challenge.
- **Collaborative participation:** teams take turns and can use limited clues; the challenge rewards reasoning, not speed.

## Deployment and privacy

The site is static and can be published through GitHub Pages. It runs on one shared classroom computer or projector. No student accounts, backend, database, AI key or paid external service is needed. Session data stays in browser memory until a team-level CSV recap is downloaded. Voice uses the browser/device speech engine for text-to-speech; it does not listen to students or recognise spoken answers. Students still select answers. Visual models run locally.

## What it does not claim

WonderLab does not replace the teacher, textbook, practical laboratory or the school's full syllabus. The two chapter pathways must be checked against the school's board and textbook edition. The simulations are simplified teaching models, not scientific instruments. The platform is designed to reduce teacher narration and routine explanation. It has not yet been shown to reduce total teacher workload or improve attainment; both claims require a real classroom evaluation.

## How a school can evaluate it

Run a small pilot. Use a short concept explanation task before and after the chapter, plus a delayed check where practical. Review the lesson checks and team-answer patterns, observe whether participation is spread across students, and record total preparation, delivery and follow-up time. Compare with the school's usual method where feasible. Report limits and unexpected results honestly.
