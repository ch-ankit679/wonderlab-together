# WonderLab Together — Multimedia Learning Guide (6.0)

Each lesson has a larger visual workbench designed around the concept. Students can make a prediction, use a control, observe the motion and compare it with the explanation. In Edition 6.0, Pip reads lesson instructions and key content aloud, narrates available lab observations after changes, and reads quiz questions and feedback. Use the fixed voice bar to replay, pause, stop, choose an installed voice and change the speaking rate.

## Cell: Structure and Functions

| Lesson | Visual experience | Labels and key learning |
|---|---|---|
| The Tiny Building Blocks | Zoom from a whole plant to a leaf-tissue cross-section and a plant cell | Organism; upper epidermis; palisade layer; spongy layer; lower epidermis; cell wall; cell membrane; chloroplast; large vacuole; nucleus. |

## Motion design

Motion is used to show a scientific process: scale zooming; organelle breathing; particles moving through a membrane; energy sparks; the bridge cart travelling; deck deflection; water waves; particles dispersing, settling or floating; and the water level rising. The site respects `prefers-reduced-motion` and disables non-essential motion where requested.

## Limits

All visual scenes simplify real systems. The animal and plant cells are not exact-scale microscopy. The membrane model shows selected transport routes, not every mechanism. The bridge model is qualitative, not a real load rating. The energy meter is a comparative illustration, not a measured biological rate. The measuring cylinder is not calibrated lab equipment. Voice narration depends on the browser/device speech engine. It provides text-to-speech only; learners still click/select answers and the platform does not listen to microphone input or grade spoken answers. See `DIAGRAM-ACCURACY-AUDIT.md` for the labels and source references.
