# WonderLab Together — diagram and label accuracy audit (5.0)

This document records the labels used in the interactive diagrams and the science checks applied during the visual rebuild. The diagrams are simplified Class 8 learning models, not complete histology or engineering plates.

## Cell: Structure and Functions

| Lesson / diagram | Labels shown | Accuracy notes |
|---|---|---|
| The Tiny Building Blocks | Organism; leaf tissue; upper epidermis; palisade layer; spongy layer; lower epidermis; plant cell; cell wall; cell membrane; chloroplast; large vacuole; nucleus | The zoom moves from a whole plant to leaf tissue and then one plant cell. The tissue view distinguishes the upper and lower epidermis, palisade and spongy layers. |
| Meet the Cell Team | Nucleus; mitochondrion; cell membrane; cytoplasm; vesicle / small vacuole | The image is a 3D animal-cell cutaway. Labels are separate interactive HTML controls. The vesicle callout is worded cautiously: animal cells may contain small vacuoles, and vesicles are small membrane-bound sacs; it is not labelled as a large plant-style central vacuole. |
| The Cell’s Smart Boundary | Hydrophilic heads; hydrophobic tails; phospholipid bilayer; channel protein; carrier protein; outside the cell; cytoplasm | The bilayer heads face watery surroundings; the hydrophobic tails face inward. Oxygen is shown diffusing through the lipid region; water is shown using a water channel (osmosis); glucose uses a carrier (facilitated diffusion). A large particle is not shown passing straight through the bilayer. |
| Powering the Cell | Outer membrane; inner membrane folds (cristae); matrix | The mitochondrion cutaway shows an outer membrane and a folded inner membrane. The energy meter is explicitly a relative teaching model, not a measured biological rate. |
| Inside a Plant Cell | Cell wall; cell membrane; cytoplasm; nucleus; chloroplasts; large central vacuole | The cell wall is outside the cell membrane. Chloroplasts and the large central vacuole are plant-cell features; those labels are removed when the student switches to the animal-cell view. |
| Cells with Special Jobs | Dendrites; cell body; axon; long muscle fibre / striations; biconcave disc; no nucleus (mature human red blood cell) | The red-blood-cell label is qualified to mature human cells. The image is a biconcave disc, not a nucleated round cell. |

| Become a Property Detective | Soft; porous; absorbent; hard; rigid; lustrous; flexible; elastic; conductive; malleable | Properties are attached to the named sample. A polished steel spoon is described as lustrous; a rubber sample as elastic/flexible; sponge as porous and absorbent. |

Sand remains insoluble and usually settles; chalk powder is insoluble and can cloud water; sawdust often floats. Floating/settling/clouding are not described as dissolving. |

## Visual and label limitations

The animal and plant cell illustrations are educational cutaways and are not drawn to exact scale. The interactive hotspots point to representative structures. The membrane transport routes simplify a more complex process; real water movement can occur through aquaporins and through the lipid bilayer, while the movement of glucose depends on specific transport proteins and the concentration/energy conditions. The bridge, energy and dissolving scenes are conceptual models, not experimental data.
