# WonderLab Together — chapter coverage and curriculum reference (5.0)

## How to read this document

WonderLab's two learning paths reorganise the science concepts present in the two preserved source chapters into six teach–practise–check lessons per chapter. The mapping below is a content crosswalk, not a claim that a particular board has approved the product or that these two topics represent a complete school-year syllabus.

Teachers should check the exact textbook edition, board syllabus, grade level and term sequence before assigning a chapter. The two pathways do not replace textbook reading, practical work or teacher explanation.

## Source chapters preserved

`data/chapters.js` is the untouched source file containing:

- **Cell: Structure and Functions** — cell basics, main cell parts, membrane movement, mitochondria and energy, plant-cell features, and specialised cells.
- **Materials Around Us** — natural sources, material properties, material selection, light transmission, dissolving in water, mass and volume.

The expanded explanations, vocabulary, lesson examples, concept checks and Wonder League question bank are in `data/learning-paths.js`.

## Cell: Structure and Functions

| Lesson | Concepts taught | Source mission connection | Learning evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The Tiny Building Blocks | Cells as the basic units of living things; cell as the smallest unit carrying out life processes; microscope | `cell-scout` | Student identifies the cell as the basic living unit. |
| 2. Meet the Cell Team | Nucleus, cell membrane, cytoplasm, mitochondria and vacuole | `cell-scout`, `cell-builder` | Student connects each structure with its main function. |
| 3. The Cell’s Smart Boundary | Cell membrane; useful materials entering; waste leaving | `cell-gates` | Student explains the two directions of exchange. |
| 4. Powering the Cell | Energy for cell activities; role of mitochondria | `cell-power` | Student explains that mitochondria help release energy from food. |
| 5. Inside a Plant Cell | Cell wall, chloroplasts, chlorophyll, cell sap and large vacuole | `cell-plant` | Student distinguishes the cell wall from the membrane and explains the role of chloroplasts. |
| 6. Cells with Special Jobs | Nerve, muscle and red blood cell structure–function relationships | `cell-rescue` | Student connects a visible cell feature with its job. |

## Materials Around Us

| Lesson | Concepts taught | Source mission connection | Learning evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Where Do Materials Come From? | Materials; plant, animal and Earth sources; processing wood pulp into paper and sand into glass | `materials-scout` | Student traces a material to a plausible natural source or process. |
| 2. Become a Property Detective | Hardness, softness, lustre and classification | `materials-sort` | Student classifies an item using an identified property. |
| 3. Choose the Right Material | Strength, stiffness, loads and design trade-offs such as weight and cost | `materials-bridge` | Student justifies a material choice using properties and the job. |
| 4. Can Light Pass Through? | Transparent, translucent and opaque | `materials-light` | Student classifies materials by how light passes through. |
| 5. The Mystery of Dissolving | Soluble and insoluble; solution; floating, settling and cloudy mixtures | `materials-mix` | Student distinguishes dissolving from other observations in water. |
| 6. Mass, Volume and Matter | Matter; mass; volume; millilitres; water displacement | `materials-matter` | Student distinguishes mass from volume and calculates a water-level rise. |

## Explicit limits

- The Cell pathway covers the cell concepts in the preserved source chapter. It is not a complete treatment of all microorganisms, bacterial-cell diversity or every cell process that may appear in a wider syllabus.
- The Materials pathway covers the concepts in the preserved source chapter. It does not claim to teach every topic involving matter, mixtures, separation techniques, optics or measurement that may be present elsewhere in a textbook or board syllabus.
- The platform currently covers only these two chapters. It does not provide a full Class 8 science course.
- The question checks are formative practice. They have not been standardised or validated as a formal assessment.
